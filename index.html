The problem

Trucks, work zones, and three sets of people who can’t see each other

Commercial heavy-duty vehicles account for roughly one third of fatal work-zone crashes nationwide. The risk is compounded by a coordination gap: planners, traffic operators and field crews each hold a different piece of the picture, and the richest data of all sits locked inside the trucks.

1 in 3fatal work-zone crashes nationwide involve a heavy-duty vehicle
1,216work-zone crashes in Pennsylvania2023
689people injured in those crashes2023
22people killed in those crashes2023

Planners look down the corridor

Maintenance is prioritised from long-term corridor assessments, with little visibility into the day-to-day difficulties that operators, field workers and drivers actually run into.

Operators look at the incident

Traffic operators and work-zone managers lack the corridor perspective, so they respond to incidents reactively instead of managing traffic to accommodate what the work zone needs next.

The trucks see everything — and tell nobody

Telematics and vision data from smart tractors and trailers capture barrier placement, lane widths and traffic disruption at close range, yet stay siloed from traffic management systems.

The approach

From crash cleanup to physics-based risk verification

The project deploys a Safety & Mobility Verification Digital Twin (SMVDT). It fuses proprietary telematics from Clarience Technologies with real-time infrastructure feeds from Cranberry Township Public Works — and potentially PennDOT — to quantify safety risks such as underride exposure alongside mobility impacts such as delay, on the same road segments and in the same time windows.

Crucially, the system does not only monitor current conditions. It verifies whether a specific vehicle configuration can safely navigate an evolving work-zone geometry.

A library of evolving work-zone and HDV risk scenarios

Collect operational data from Cranberry Township Public Works and define rigorous safety metrics for the ways heavy vehicles actually get into trouble in a work zone.

A dual testbed for validation against reality

Telematics and smart-trailer camera data are collected across two very different networks, then used to validate physics-informed machine learning models against the rare-but-deadly cases that a purely statistical model never sees.

  • CTPW municipal network — 140+ miles
  • Clarience instrumented route — 185 miles

A communication risk detection interface

Communication between the CTPW Transportation Operations Center and work-zone engineering and management teams is collected and mapped against protocol, exposing reasoning gaps — places where operational directives and safety standards quietly diverge.

  • Section level

Two maps that finally share an axis

Safety is measured with trailer-to-vehicle clearance: minimum rear and side gaps, the rate of clearance violations, and time below threshold. Mobility is measured with delay, queue length, throughput and speed variation. Because both are computed on the same segments and time windows, the project can produce a Safety Map, a Mobility Map, and a Combined Safety–Mobility Map with simple scores that show whether a change reduces risk, delay, or both.

End-to-end safety metrics

Six ways a heavy truck gets into trouble — each one made measurable

The framework’s first job is arithmetic: turn each of these failure modes into a number that can be computed from telematics and work-zone geometry, compared across corridors, and audited by an agency.

Underride exposure

Clearance between a trailer and the vehicle behind or beside it — minimum rear and side gaps, the rate of clearance violations, and time spent below threshold.

Blind-zone conflicts

Occupancy of the zones a driver cannot see, from the cab of a specific tractor–trailer configuration, at the moment a worker or vehicle is in them.

Merge & queue risk

Queue-end collision exposure and blind-side merge conflict at a closure, where the back of a stopped queue meets traffic still running at speed.

Stability anomalies

Braking, roll and articulation events that mark a loss of control margin before anything visible goes wrong.

Intrusion probability

The likelihood that a vehicle enters the protected work space, given the geometry actually in place and the traffic state actually observed.

Barrier strike from off-tracking

A trailer sweeping wider than its tractor through a taper or curve — the case where a legal lane width is still not a survivable one for this vehicle.

And the other axis: mobility

Delay, queue length, throughput and speed variation, computed on the same segments and the same time windows as the safety metrics — which is what lets a change be scored as reducing risk, delay, or both.

Why it has to be physics-informed

The cases that matter most are the ones that have barely happened. Physics-informed machine learning generates them, PennSTART verifies them against real vehicle behaviour, and only then are they mapped back onto live corridor traces.

Where the work happens

A dual testbed: one municipality, one commercial freight route

Two networks with genuinely different traffic, geometry and governance — which is what makes the validation meaningful rather than site-specific.

140+ miles

Cranberry Township Public Works

Municipal network positioned at the junction of I-76, I-79, US-19 and PA-228 — a dense interchange of interstate, US and state routes feeding local streets.

  • Live infrastructure and work-zone feeds
  • Transportation Operations Center communication data
  • Municipal corridor management deployment site
185miles

Clarience Technologies instrumented route

A commercial testing route running through PennDOT Regions 2 and 3, carrying production freight with instrumented tractors and trailers.

  • Braking, stability, steering and lighting events
  • Tractor–trailer articulation angles
  • Smart-trailer camera feeds

PennSTART

Synthetic rare-event scenarios generated by the twin are physically validated at the PennSTART test track, so the simulation is checked against real vehicle physics before anything is mapped back onto live corridors.

Deployment plan

Twelve months, four quarters, four deliverables

Q1
Months 1–3

Digital infrastructure & metric definition

Data ingestion & fusion. Ingest historical and real-time telematics — braking, stability, trailer camera feeds — from the Clarience 185-mile route, and map the geometry of the CTPW 140-mile network and PennDOT corridors such as Route 228, creating the twin’s base layer.

Metric formulation. Mathematically define the end-to-end safety metrics: underride exposure, blind-zone conflicts, merge/queue risk, stability anomalies, intrusion probability and barrier-strike risk from off-tracking.

DeliverableInitial SMVDT architecture specification and safety-metrics definition document.

Q2
Months 4–6

System integration & simulation kickoff

SMVDT v1. Fuse the data layers into a twin that can overlay dynamic vehicle data onto static work-zone maps and output traffic-context-adaptive maps.

Rare-event generation. Use physics-informed machine learning to generate synthetic edge cases — blind-side merges, nighttime barrier shadows — that are statistically rare but operationally catastrophic.

Cognitive analysis. Record exchanges between fleet dispatchers, TOC operators and field crews, mine text from traffic management protocols, and identify where directives deviate from safety standards.

DeliverableSMVDT v1 prototype and initial rare-event scenario bank.

Q3
Months 7–9

Validation & stress testing

PennSTART verification. Physically validate the generated rare-event scenarios at the test track so the simulation matches real-world vehicle physics.

Simulation-to-road transfer. Map validated scenarios back onto real traces from the Clarience route and the CTPW network to locate risk hotspots at network, section and work-zone levels.

Interface development. Build the Communication Risk Detection Interface, with alerts aligned to CTPW and PennDOT Temporary Traffic Control standards.

DeliverableValidated simulation library and draft Communication Risk Detection Interface.

Q4
Months 10–12

Deployment, handoff & policy

Field deployment. Deploy the SMVDT and the Communication Risk Detection Interface with CTPW for municipal corridor management and PennDOT for active work-zone monitoring.

Public dataset release. Publish the anonymised Safety–Mobility Dataset to support broader research into HDV safety.

Policy handoff. Deliver a policy brief covering verification standards, routing recommendations and procurement guidelines for verification-ready telematics.

DeliverableLive SMVDT dashboard, Communication Risk Detection Interface, final policy brief and public dataset.

Outputs & impacts

What exists at the end of the year that does not exist now

Software

Safety–Mobility Verification Digital Twin

A system that fuses proprietary smart-trailer telematics with dynamic infrastructure data, powering a Corridor Safety Dashboard deployed with Cranberry Township and PennDOT that generates traffic-context-adaptive maps for commercial fleet routing.

Software

Communication Risk Detection Interface

A tool that identifies reasoning gaps between Traffic Operations Centers and field crews, restructuring safety alerts so they align with cognitive limits and agency standard operating procedures.

Data

Rare-Event Simulation Library

A curated database of physics-informed edge cases — blind-side merges, barrier shadows — validated at PennSTART rather than assumed.

Data

Public Safety–Mobility Dataset

The region’s first public safety–mobility dataset, anonymised and released to support open-source algorithm development — and to let third-party navigation providers incorporate verified safety constraints.

Method

End-to-End Safety Metrics Framework

Novel algorithms quantifying underride exposure, stability anomalies and off-tracking barrier strikes, supporting a new Open Verification Protocol for validating autonomous-ready freight systems.

Process

Human–AI Communication Playbook & policy brief

A playbook that restructures safety alerts around how crews actually work, plus a policy brief for statewide scaling of verification standards and procurement guidance.

Expected impacts

Practice

A repeatable verification protocol that moves agencies from reactive crash analysis to proactive risk auditing, informing PennDOT and municipal policy on barrier placement, routing and procurement specs for verification-ready telematics.

Safety

Measurable reductions in barrier-strike risk from off-tracking, queue-end collision exposure, blind-side merge conflicts, intrusion and underride probability — with targets for reduced excessive speeding (>11 mph over the limit) and surrogate conflict rates against pre-pilot baselines.

Reliability

A higher actionability rate for safety data: warnings mapped directly onto existing traffic management and Temporary Traffic Control standards, reducing cognitive load and shortening median acknowledgment times for municipal crews and drivers.

Where this sits in the literature

The gap this closes

A review of TRID shows strong existing work on work-zone operations (queue warning, late-merge control), truck safety technology (camera and alert systems, leader–follower truck-mounted attenuators, platooning) and simulation tools. But most studies analyse safety and traffic delay separately, are limited to a single device or site, or do not use real heavy-truck and smart-trailer data.

This project closes that gap at the corridor level: tractor and trailer telematics are fused with work-zone layout, lane status, traffic state and weather so that safety and mobility are computed on the same road segments and time windows — producing a practical, deployment-ready corridor tool that agencies, fleets and drivers can use to select routes, set work-zone plans, and demonstrate measurable improvement.
