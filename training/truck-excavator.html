Who moves, when, and who said so

      Signals and radio protocol

      Site protocol used in this training: use closed-loop communication for vehicle movements: a clear primary instruction (usually radio) and, where the site plan calls for it, a confirming horn, hand signal, light or visual acknowledgement. These horn codes, read-backs and vehicle hand signals are site-specific procedures, not national OSHA signal codes. The crew must be trained on the procedure actually used at the site before movements begin.

      Six site rules used in this training

      1. The excavator operator controls the loading area.Trucks wait at staging until called in. Nobody enters the swing radius on assumption.
      2. Closed-loop read-back (site rule).For this training scenario, the receiver repeats movement instructions before acting. OSHA does not prescribe a universal read-back for excavator–truck movements; this is the project's communication control.
      3. Lost contact means stop.If the driver loses sight of the spotter, or radio goes quiet, the machine stops and waits.
      4. Anyone may call “All stop” under this site's stop-work policy.Operators stop first and the crew resolves the hazard before resuming. This is a site safety rule; crane-specific OSHA stop-signal requirements are separate.
      5. Eye contact before approach.Ground workers approach a machine only after the operator has seen them, acknowledged, and grounded the bucket.
      6. One spotter per vehicle.The driver follows one designated signal person only, except for an emergency stop, which is obeyed from anyone.

      Horn signals supplement the radio; they are not a substitute for it on a noisy site. Confirm that horns and reverse alarms are audible above surrounding noise at the actual loading position.

      EmergencyAll stop, all stop, all stop. Every unit replies with call sign and stopped.

      Say who you are calling, then who you are, then the message. Keep a dedicated channel for the loading area and do a radio check at the start of every shift.

      Danger zones around the loading point

      Most struck-by and caught-between incidents at loading points happen in a handful of predictable places. Mark them on the ground and on the traffic management plan.

      R+Swing radius

      Full reach of the boom plus tail swing of the counterweight, with a buffer set by the site plan. No ground personnel inside while the machine is live. Barricade it where foot traffic is possible.

      0People under the bucket

      OSHA 1926.651(e) prohibits employees beneath loads handled by lifting or digging equipment and requires employees to stand away from vehicles being loaded. This training also uses the conservative site rule that the bucket does not pass over the truck cab.

      2Truck blind sides

      Directly behind the truck and along the passenger side. A spotter stands on the driver’s side, visible in the mirror, never in the reversing path.

      2 ftExcavation edge

      Spoil and equipment kept at least 2 ft back (1926.651(j)(2)). Use stop logs, berms or barricades where trucks approach an edge without a clear view (1926.651(f)).

      10 ftOverhead power lines

      Under OSHA 1926.600(a)(6), equipment working or travelling near energized lines keeps at least 10 ft clearance for lines rated 50 kV or less, with greater clearance for higher voltages unless the line is de-energized and visibly grounded. Watch booms and raised bodies. Showing lines and controlled clearances on the site plan is a project planning control, not wording from this OSHA paragraph.

      360°Excavator rear

      The counterweight swings behind the tracks where the operator cannot see. Workers on foot stay out of the tail-swing area and never walk between the machine and a fixed object.

      Roles and responsibilities

      Each person owns specific decisions in the loading cycle. Inspectors should be able to ask anyone on the crew “who tells the truck to move?” and get the same answer.

      Excavator operator

      Owns: when trucks enter, where they stop, when they leave

      • Calls trucks in from staging and releases them when loaded.
      • Holds the bucket clear of the truck path during spotting.
      • Swings over the rear or side of the bed, never over the cab or people.
      • Stops swinging if anyone enters the swing radius.
      • Grounds the bucket whenever the machine is idle or the operator leaves the seat.

      Truck driver

      Owns: their vehicle’s movement within the signals given

      • Stops at staging and waits to be called. Uses the site's closed-loop read-back procedure.
      • Reverses under the applicable backup-alarm/observer requirement and the site's backing procedure.
      • Sets the parking brake and keeps hands visible during loading.
      • For a covered haulage vehicle loaded by power equipment, the required cab shield/canopy must be present under 1926.601(b)(6). Under 1926.651(e), an operator may remain in a compliant protected cab; if the site procedure places the driver in a designated safe area instead, the move is completed before loading begins.
      • Checks and secures the load in the designated area before entering public roads, as applicable.

      Spotter / signal person

      Owns: guiding a reversing truck safely

      • Trained, wearing hi-vis, briefed on the site signals.
      • Stands on the driver’s side, visible in the mirror, outside the truck path and the swing radius.
      • Does no other task while spotting: no phone, no shovel.
      • Gives the stop signal the moment anything is uncertain.

      Ground personnel

      Owns: their own position on site

      • Stay out of the swing radius and the haul road.
      • Approach a machine only after eye contact and acknowledgement from the operator.
      • Use designated walkways; never walk behind a reversing vehicle.

      Supervisor / competent person

      Owns: the plan, the briefing, and stop-work decisions

      • Writes and maintains the traffic management plan and JHA.
      • Assigns radio channel, call signs and spotters each shift.
      • Holds the pre-task briefing and records attendance.
      • Inspects the excavation daily and after rain or other changes.

      Inspector

      Owns: verification that plan, people and equipment match

      • Checks that documents exist and match what is actually on the ground.
      • Observes at least one full loading cycle, including signals and read-backs.
      • Tests alarms and horns at the loading position.
      • Records findings and follows up on corrective actions.

      Regulations and standards

      Summaries written for training, with the point an inspector should check. Federal OSHA is the baseline; state-plan states (for example Cal/OSHA) may be stricter. MSHA rules replace OSHA at mines, quarries and many borrow pits. DOT rules apply once a truck uses public roads.
