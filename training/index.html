Call & Signal Interactive safety training

Who moves, when, and who said so

Four browser-based training environments built around real work-zone and construction scenarios. Each one puts you in a role, makes you commit to a decision under time pressure, and then shows you what the decision cost — with the underlying OSHA, MUTCD, FMCSA and NIOSH material cited in place.

Pick a module About the research project

Nothing here is a substitute for site-specific training, a lift plan, or a traffic control plan. These are teaching models.

The modules

Four scenarios, four kinds of mistake

Run them in any order. The two 3D trainers share a scenario engine: review the briefing, then choose Practice to run the shift yourself or Watch Demo to see it played correctly first.

Roadway work zone safety

Highway lane closure and two-lane flagging. Run the tailgate meeting, set and later move the taper, pick a flagging station, stop a moving line of traffic, and survive a driver who runs your stop and a radio that dies mid-shift.

  • 3D · interactive
  • Lane closure
  • Flagging
  • Intrusion response
  • MUTCD 11th Ed.
Start training →

Highway construction communication

Two scenarios on one channel: a girder delivery by crane, and loading out a widening cut with an excavator. Decide where the driver waits, what to do when someone walks into the swing path, and who wins when two voices give orders at once.

  • 3D · interactive
  • Crane lifts
  • Excavator load-out
  • Radio discipline
  • OSHA 1926
Start training →

Crane siting exercise

A draggable, scaled site plan for a building site and two bridge spans. Move the crane, the trailers and the loads; watch radius, geometry flags, production rate, elapsed time and illustrative cost update together — and see when a tower crane beats a mobile at the same position.

  • 2D site plan
  • Lift-by-lift costing
  • Mobile vs tower
  • Geometry screening
  • Printable sheet
Start exercise →

Truck–excavator communication

One full loading cycle, animated in plan view and broken down step by step: closed-loop radio calls, the horn code, spotter hand signals, the swing-radius exclusion zone, operator blind spots, the paperwork that should exist on site, an inspector field checklist and a knowledge check.

  • Animated plan view
  • Radio protocol
  • Hand signals
  • Danger zones
  • Inspector checklist

How to use these

Built to be argued with

Every scenario forces a choice that a real crew has to make, then holds the consequence open for discussion. Source material is cited inline — OSHA 29 CFR 1926, the MUTCD 11th Edition, 49 CFR for motor carriers, and NIOSH struck-by and equipment-visibility work — so a crew can check the reasoning rather than take the scoring on faith.

  • Runs entirely in the browser — nothing to install, no account
  • Light and dark themes, keyboard accessible, works on a tablet
  • Where the module distinguishes a site rule from a legal requirement, it says so explicitly

Why this feeds the research

The Safety21 project’s third pillar is a Communication Risk Detection Interface: a tool that finds the reasoning gaps between a Traffic Operations Center and the crew in the work zone. These modules are where those gaps are first made visible and teachable — the same closed-loop discipline, the same stop-work authority, the same question of who has the authority to move a machine.
